The RSS Renaissance: Why Indie Readers Are Thriving Again

It's 2026 and RSS is having a moment. After years of being dismissed as a relic of the early web, a quiet resurgence is underway — driven by reader fatigue with algorithmic timelines, a renewed interest in the open web, and a crop of beautifully crafted native apps that make following your favourite sites a genuine pleasure.

NetNewsWire sits at the centre of that revival. Free, open-source, and built by a team that cares about both craft and privacy, it has become the go-to feed reader for people who want something that simply gets out of the way and lets them read.

Why Algorithms Pushed Readers Back to RSS

Social platforms promise to surface the content you care about, but the reality is a feed shaped by engagement metrics and an opaque ranking system. RSS is the antidote: a simple contract between publisher and reader where nothing is filtered, reordered, or withheld.

"An RSS reader is the only place on the web where chronological order is still considered a feature, not a bug."

The signal-to-noise improvement alone is compelling. Once you curate your own subscription list, you read what you chose to read — and only that.
